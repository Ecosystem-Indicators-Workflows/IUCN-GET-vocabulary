The **Global Ecosystem Typology** vocabulary is a beta release of the classification structure underpinning the IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology (GET), the first internationally agreed framework for describing and mapping the world's ecosystems. It has been published in Research Vocabularies Australia (RVA) as part of the Ecosystem Indicator Workflows project, giving Australian and international researchers a citable, machine-readable version of the typology's hierarchy for use in data description, discovery and interoperability.

The IUCN GET was initially developed between 2017 and 2022 through a global consultation an intensive peer review process coordinated by the IUCN Commission on Ecosystem Management, led by Professor David Keith (UNSW) and Professor Emily Nicholson (formerly Deakin University, currently at University of Melbourne). It classifies ecosystems by their functional and compositional characteristics across six hierarchical levels. The upper three levels group ecosystems by shared ecological processes: five core realms (terrestrial, freshwater, marine, subterranean and atmospheric), six transitional realms, 25 functional biomes, and 110 ecosystem functional groups. The lower three levels (regional ecosystem subgroups, global ecosystem types and subglobal ecosystem types) distinguish ecosystems with similar function but different species assemblages, supporting finer-scale regional and local application. The framework was formally described in an IUCN publication (2020, version 2.0) and published in *Nature* (2022) as version 2.1.

This vocabulary was released in beta in May 2026 for community testing and feedback, and is not yet the final production version. It is being developed as a foundational milestone of the [Ecosystem Indicator Workflows](https://ardc.edu.au/project/ecosystem-indicator-workflows/) project (2026 to 2028), led by Dr José R. Ferrer-Paris at the UNSW Centre for Ecosystem Science, in partnership with the ARDC, James Cook University, QCIF Digital Research, the University of Melbourne and the Queensland University of Technology. The project is building a modular, FAIR-aligned toolbox of vocabularies, datasets and code to support the development of ecosystem-specific indicators, and sits within the ARDC's broader [Environmental Indicators Initiative](https://ardc.edu.au/article/measuring-what-matters-ardc-launches-the-environmental-indicators-initiative/).

A machine-actionable typology of this kind gives ecosystem accounting, IUCN Red List of Ecosystems risk assessments, Green Status of Ecosystems recovery assessments, and State of the Environment reporting a consistent way to classify and cross-reference ecosystems. It supports Australia's obligations under the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and underpins the Ecosystem Indicator Workflows project's aim of enabling transparent, reproducible indicator development across Australia's diverse ecosystems.

## Related resources

- **Explore the typology:** the [IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology website](https://global-ecosystems.org/explore) provides the authoritative reference structure, descriptive profiles and maps for all realms, biomes and functional groups.
- **Governance and updates:** the [GET data hub](https://red-list-ecosystem.github.io/GET-data-hub/) maintained by the IUCN Red List of Ecosystems Thematic Group documents the governance process, nomination process and public register of proposed changes to the typology, which this vocabulary will track as it matures.
- **Project background:** the [Ecosystem Indicator Workflows project page](https://ardc.edu.au/project/ecosystem-indicator-workflows/) (ARDC) and [project website](https://ecosystem-indicators-workflows.github.io/) describe the wider toolbox of vocabularies, datasets, code and community workshops this vocabulary contributes to.
- **Initiative context:** [Measuring What Matters](https://ardc.edu.au/article/measuring-what-matters-ardc-launches-the-environmental-indicators-initiative/) (ARDC, August 2026) explains how this vocabulary fits within the ARDC's Environmental Indicators Initiative.
